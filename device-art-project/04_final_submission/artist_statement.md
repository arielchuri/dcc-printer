# Artist Statement: The Electromechanics of Disposable Mortality

**Project:** DCC Printer (Thermal Tabletop Oracle)  
**Artist / Designer:** Ariel Churi  
**Course:** PSAM 2230 Device Art (Parsons School of Design, Fall 2026)

---

## The Machine as Altar

In contemporary digital culture, randomness is disembodied. We tap glass screens, generate synthetic seeds on remote cloud servers, and consume infinite procedural variations without friction or consequence. The digital realm has stripped fate of its ceremony.

The **DCC Printer** is an intervention against this disembodied frictionlessness. Housed in a matte black and cast acrylic chassis, the device is an autonomous electromechanical oracle dedicated to the cult ritual of the **"0-Level Character Funnel"** from the tabletop roleplaying game *Dungeon Crawl Classics*.

In the funnel, players do not begin as chosen heroes; they begin as fragile, randomized medieval peasants—turnip peddlers, ditch diggers, gong farmers—flung into meat-grinder subterranean labyrinths where death is sudden, absurd, and overwhelming. The sole surviving peasant inherits the mantle of an adventurer; the rest are buried in the dark.

The *DCC Printer* translates this philosophy of **procedural fate and disposable mortality** into the physical vocabulary of **Device Art**:

### 1. The Poetics of Ephemeral Receipt Paper
Instead of displaying stats on an OLED screen or generating a PDF character sheet, the machine renders its prophecies onto standard 58mm thermal receipt paper. Thermal paper is the quintessential currency of disposable commerce: thin, fragile, chemically coated, and doomed to fade with exposure to ultraviolet light and heat. By printing a character’s identity, stats, and birth augur on thermal paper, the object immediately confronts the player with the fragility of existence. When that character perishes 20 minutes into the game, the receipt is burned, torn in half, or pocketed as a faded relic—giving tangible physical weight to the digital calculation.

### 2. The Weight of Tactile Controls
Every interaction with the *DCC Printer* is deliberately mechanical:
- The **Alpha SR16A rotary switch** does not glide smoothly; it snaps into its 8 detents with a heavy, industrial mechanical "clack," forcing the user to commit to a table mode.
- The **arcade triangle pushbutton** pulses with a sinusoidal breathing glow, simulating a biological resting state that shifts into a frantic strobe when triggered.
- The **Cashino thermal print head** does not print silently; it whirrs and vibrates, mechanically spewing out grotesque ASCII demon skulls, ability rolls, and timestamps.

### 3. Autonomy from the Cloud
The machine is completely self-contained. Driven by a dual-core Raspberry Pi Pico running CircuitPython, it requires no internet connection, no smartphone app, and no telemetry. It is an honest machine—an appliance of doom, a pocket-sized bureau of procedural mortality.

In *Device Art*, the mechanism itself is the concept, and the device is the artwork. The *DCC Printer* asks players to surrender their agency not to a screen, but to an electromechanical oracle that calculates their doom and hands it to them on a slip of warm paper.
